resumen: "On November 5, the Austin City Council voted to update the Strategic Housing Blueprint with affordability and preservation goals through 2037, after the current 2017 plan fell short of its target of 60,000 income-restricted units."
---

The Austin City Council voted on **November 5** to formally kick off the update of the **Strategic Housing Blueprint**, the plan that sets the city's goals for building and preserving affordable housing. The new plan will cover **2028–2037** and, unlike its predecessor, will place explicit weight on preserving existing housing, not just on new construction.

## Context

The current plan, adopted in 2017, set a goal of adding at least **60,000 income-restricted units by 2028**. A late-2023 report from the city's Housing Department had already noted that this goal needed to be revised to reflect current market conditions, and progress has been particularly weak at the lowest income level (30% or less of the area median income).

The shift toward preservation responds to a recent finding by city staff: Austin has identified more than **550 naturally occurring affordable housing (NOAH) properties**, totaling **68,311 units** — about **28% of the city's entire rental inventory**. That inventory carries no formal income restriction, which makes it vulnerable to demolition or renovation that takes it out of affordable range without any current policy capturing it. The Council also asked that the update include specific reporting on NOAH units lost to demolition or conversion tied to the city's incentive programs, and that the "Opportunity Map" the city uses to direct those incentives geographically be updated.

## What to do with this

- If you have clients who own older rental buildings with no formal income restriction (NOAH), now is the time to start talking to them about the preservation incentives that will predictably come with the update — before the Council closes the 2028–37 process.
- The "Opportunity Map" will be updated as part of this process: if you work with developers who depend on density incentives or tax exemptions tied to "high opportunity" zones, it is worth closely following changes to those geographic zones over the coming months.
- The new report on NOAH units lost to demolition or conversion will make any redevelopment project that eliminates naturally affordable rental housing more visible — and more politically sensitive — even without a formal restriction; anticipate that if you represent a developer in that kind of transaction in submarkets with high NOAH stock.
