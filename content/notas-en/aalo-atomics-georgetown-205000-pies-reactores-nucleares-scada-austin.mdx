**Aalo Atomics**, the nuclear microreactor startup, leased a **205,000-square-foot** industrial building at Georgetown's **CrossPoint Business District** to manufacture its reactors and power-generation components, The Real Deal reported. The **$25 million** construction project is scheduled to break ground in November.

## Context

The leased building — CrossPoint Building 2, at 4815 N Interstate 35 — is owned by developer **Jackson Shaw**. As the Austin Business Journal previously reported, Aalo was also in negotiations for a much larger adjacent building in the same park, at 606,000 square feet, which would point to further expansion plans along Georgetown's I-35 industrial corridor. The company, co-founded in 2023 by Matt Loszak and Yasir Arafat, has raised $100 million in venture capital this year, on top of an earlier $36 million round.

The Real Deal frames Aalo's lease within a broader trend it is documenting this week: the rise of so-called "SCADA" companies (space, chips, advanced manufacturing, defense, AI) as the engine of industrial and office absorption in the Austin area, at a time when traditional institutional investors have pulled back from the market. According to CoStar data cited by the outlet, these companies have leased 3.7 million square feet in the Austin metro, up from just 200,000 square feet in 2015. Within that same universe, The Real Deal identified 23 defense, advanced manufacturing and aerospace companies that leased 36 properties of 10,000 square feet or more over the past two years — a group that includes Blue Origin's 1.3-million-square-foot lease in Hutto, the largest in Austin's history.

## What to do with this

- If you represent industrial buildings or land for flex/industrial development along the I-35 corridor between Georgetown and Round Rock, Aalo's lease — and the possible expansion into the adjacent 606,000-square-foot building — signals active large-format demand in that specific submarket, useful as a comparable when pricing nearby industrial inventory.
- For brokers serving the advanced manufacturing and energy sectors, it is worth mapping which other defense, chip or nuclear energy startups are looking for space in Austin before competition for large buildings intensifies, following the pattern already set by Blue Origin in Hutto and Allen Control Systems in north Austin.
- If you advise investors or industrial developers, the jump from 200,000 to 3.7 million square feet leased by "SCADA" companies since 2015 suggests that this segment — more than the traditional institutional one — is what is sustaining industrial absorption in the Austin area in the near term.
