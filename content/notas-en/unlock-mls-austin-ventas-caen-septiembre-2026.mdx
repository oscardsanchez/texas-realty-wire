Closed home sales in the Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos metro fell 14.8% year over year in September 2026, to 2,223 units, according to Unlock MLS's monthly report — but the median sale price rose 1.4% over the same period, to $421,000. In the City of Austin, sales fell 12.7% to 777 units, while the median price rose 4.2% to $570,958.

## Context

The combination of fewer sales with prices that are not giving way — and even rising — is the clearest sign so far that the price adjustment of the past 13 months is losing steam in Austin, as TRERC's statewide data for June already showed. Unlock MLS attributes September's drop to buyers' sensitivity to mortgage rates, particularly first-time buyers, who are the most affected when the monthly payment rises. Pending sales, a leading indicator, fell only 1.8% year over year — far less than closed sales — and active inventory in the metro stays at 5.4 months, a level that still favors the buyer but without the supply cushion of a year ago.

## What to do with this

- If you have first-time buyers waiting "for prices to fall further," September's data contradicts that bet: the median price rose even as sales slowed. It is worth adjusting that conversation.
- For undecided sellers, 5.4 months of inventory is still manageable — it is not the 2021 seller's market, but neither is it the price collapse some clients still fear.
- The gap between pending sales (−1.8%) and closed sales (−14.8%) suggests that part of September's drop may be closing-calendar noise rather than a real slowdown in demand — confirm it with October's data before drawing definitive conclusions with a client.
- The City of Austin's median price ($570,958) remains far above the metro area's ($421,000) — useful for framing expectations with buyers who compare Austin proper with the more affordable suburbs.
