**Velo Flats**, a 70-unit complex in east Austin, and **URBN Haus**, a 31-unit one along I-35, appear on The Real Deal's November list of commercial loans flagged for foreclosure in Texas. The statewide total for this type of loan fell to **$560 million**, well below the more than $1 billion recorded in October — but Austin still has a presence of its own on the list.

## Context

Velo Flats, at 2606 Wheless Lane, is owned by **Rastegar Property Company**, Ari Rastegar's firm, which holds several properties in the Windsor Park area. The troubled loan is for **$11.3 million**, originated by **Thrive Lending** in 2022. Rastegar faces another similar case this same month — the Infinity Views office building — and has said publicly that the dispute with Thrive Lending stems from a pattern in which the lender forecloses on loans for individual properties within a larger portfolio; in the Infinity Views case, Rastegar says he reached an agreement with another creditor (Mesa Bridge Capital) before the auction. Thrive Lending did not respond in time to a request for comment from The Real Deal.

URBN Haus, at 8221 North IH-35, carries a **$10.2 million** loan. The Real Deal did not identify its owner or the lending entity in this report.

The drop in the statewide total (from more than $1 billion in October to $560 million in November) does not necessarily mean distress is easing: The Real Deal's reports from previous months have shown that many properties reappear on successive lists while negotiations with creditors are extended, rather than being resolved through sales or refinancings.

## What to do with this

- If you work multifamily in east Austin or along the I-35 corridor, it is worth monitoring the auction dates for Velo Flats and URBN Haus — they are two potential value-add acquisition opportunities before they reach the open market.
- Rastegar Property Company now has two separate assets in distress this month (Velo Flats and Infinity Views) and a public dispute with Thrive Lending — if you have contacts with investors or lenders tied to its Austin portfolio, it is a good time to ask about the rest of its properties in the area.
- Do not take the drop in the statewide total (from more than $1 billion to $560 million) as a sign that multifamily distress in Austin has been resolved: the city keeps appearing on the list month after month, just with less aggregate volume than in October.
